# Contrasting Cases — Unit 3
## Two SCAMPER grids on the same object: which is stronger, and why?

Used in **Session 11**, after the worked demo and before your own grid. The object is the
same in both: **a steel tiffin box sold by a small utensils shop.**

Read both. Decide which is stronger. **Write why before anyone tells you.**

> दोन्ही वाचा. कोणते चांगले आहे ते ठरवा. *का* ते लिहा.

## Grid B

| Prompt | Ideas |
|---|---|
| **S** Substitute | Insulated steel · copper-bottom tier for keeping dal hot · silicone seal instead of the metal clip that rusts |
| **C** Combine | Tiffin with a fold-flat spoon in the lid · tiffin + small pickle cup that clips inside · sold with a cotton carry bag stitched by the SHG next door |
| **A** Adapt | The Japanese bento idea — compartments in one tier · the pressure-cooker gasket for a proper seal · the bicycle-bell clip for a one-hand latch |
| **M** Modify | Shallow wide tier for rotis so they do not curl · two-tier for one person, not four · matte finish so it does not show fingerprints |
| **P** Put to another use | Planter (it is already sold that way in the shop's own window) · dry-goods storage · a courier box for home-made sweets sent by bus |
| **E** Eliminate | **The clip** — friction-fit tiers · **the handle** — a strap instead · **the third tier** — most customers use two · **the shine** — nobody wants to polish it |
| **R** Rearrange | Lid that becomes a plate · widest tier on top so the roti is reached first · handle that folds into the lid so it stacks in a cupboard |

## The trap in Grid A

Grid A is not lazy. It is what most people produce on a first attempt: three additive ideas,
the hard prompts skipped, nothing removed. **The skipped rows are the finding.** Eliminate and
Put-to-another-use are where the ideas nobody else has are hiding — and they are the rows most
people leave blank.

When you fill your own grid today, count your empty rows before you count your ideas.
