# Facilitator Session Guide — Template
## MANB109 Creativity and Innovations

**Every generated session guide follows this shape.** This is a studio course: you are
facilitating, not lecturing. The guide tells you what to do and — more importantly — when to
stop talking.

Copy this structure for `lectures/lecture_lec_NN.md`. Do not replace it with lecture prose.

---

# Session NN — [Title]

**Unit:** N · **Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.x, 109.2

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Recall, then warm-up

### 0:00 – 0:03 · Recall — from memory, log closed

> [Two or three questions about last session and the anchor, answered aloud to a partner]

Three minutes. Do not correct anyone — the effort of retrieving is the point, not accuracy.
Retrieval from memory is what moves last session's learning into long-term memory; rereading
the log does not. Every session opens this way from Session 2.

### 0:03 – 0:10 · Warm-up

**What you do:** [the exercise, stated as an instruction you can read aloud]

**Why it is here:** [one line — usually lowering the cost of speaking]

> Weeks 1–2: team-attributed only. Do not call on individuals yet.

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**
> [a single sentence]

**Say roughly this:** [3–5 short beats, not a script]

**Worked example, if the session introduces a technique:** build one in front of them, badly,
narrating each rule as you apply it. Students copy what they saw done, not what they were told.

**Do not:** cover the full theory. Fifteen minutes, then stop. If it will not fit in fifteen
minutes it belongs in the making phase as a discovery, not here as a telling.

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**
> [one sentence the students could repeat back]

**Applied to:** their anchor enterprise (see `templates/anchor_business_brief.md`)

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi. This is divergent work.

**Circulating — what good looks like:**
- [signal]
- [signal]

**What stuck looks like, and the nudge:**
| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| [symptom] | [question, not an answer] |
| Blank sheet after 10 min | "Tell me about the owner. Not the business — the person." |
| One idea defended hard | "Give me the worst possible version of this." |

**Do not** solve it for them. Ask a question and walk away.

**If someone finishes early:** in creative work that usually means they stopped too soon.
Hand them the extension printed on their worksheet — it always pushes further into the same
task, never onto a new one.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md`. Pitches in **English**, three minutes, structure
in `templates/pitch_structure.md`.

**Every session ends here.** This is the attainment path for CO 109.2 — if the pitch is cut
for time, the CO is not met.

## 1:50 – 2:00 · Idea log

Students fill `templates/idea_log.md` for this session. Silent. Do not talk over it.

---

## Materials the student holds

Every session has a **worksheet** (`worksheets/worksheet_lec_NN.md`) — recall box, the task,
scaffolded space for each making phase, the extension, pitch prep, and an after-critique box.
Sessions that introduce a technique also use a **contrasting-cases sheet** so students see a
strong and a weak example before they produce their own.

## Forward link — say it out loud

> "You will use this again in [MANB3xxA] when you [do X]."

Signposted transfer is retained; unsignposted transfer usually is not.

## If you are short on time

Cut from **input**, never from making or pitch. The lecture beat is the only part of a studio
session that is safe to lose.

## If the room is small (under 10) / large (over 40)

- **Small:** every student pitches every session.
- **Large:** rotate — five pitch each session, everyone pitches at least three times across
  the semester. Track it; do not let the confident students absorb all the slots.
