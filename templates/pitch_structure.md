# Three-Minute Pitch Structure
## MANB109 Creativity and Innovations

Every session ends with a share-out of about three minutes. You will do this **15 times**
this semester. That repetition is the point — it is the single most placement-relevant skill
this course gives you.

Delivered in **English**. Prepared in whatever language you think in.

---

## The shape

| Beat | Time | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The person** | 30 s | Who runs this business, and one concrete detail that makes them real |
| **2. The stuck** | 30 s | What is not working — in their words, not yours |
| **3. The idea** | 60 s | What you propose. One idea, not five |
| **4. The picture** | 30 s | Hold up the sheet. Point at it. Do not read it aloud |
| **5. The ask** | 30 s | What you want from the room: a decision, a critique, a missing piece |

## Rules

- **One idea.** Five ideas at three minutes each is thirty-six seconds per idea. Pick one.
- **Show the artefact.** Your mind map or SCAMPER grid is the visual. Hold it up.
- **Numbers beat adjectives.** "Forty customers a day" lands; "many customers" does not.
- **Stop at three minutes**, mid-sentence if necessary. Finishing on time is part of the skill.

## Preparing

Say it aloud once before you present. Silently rehearsing does not find the sentence you
cannot say. Time yourself — three minutes is shorter than it feels.

## If you freeze

Everyone does, early on. Go back to beat 1 and describe the person. Describing a real human
you know is always easier than defending an idea, and it restarts the pitch.
