# Session 12 — SCAMPER for Product Improvement

**Unit:** 3 · SCAMPER Technique for Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.3, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> The method meets the anchor's actual offering. Eliminate is given deliberate weight because it is the prompt that produces the ideas an owner has not already had.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "What Could They Stop Doing"

**What you do.**

> "Two minutes. What could your anchor business stop doing entirely tomorrow and lose nothing?"

Take five answers. Push on each: "Really nothing?"

**Why it is here.** Puts the hardest prompt first, while the room is fresh, and frames subtraction as the session's theme.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Most improvements are additions. The best ones are usually subtractions.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The object today is your anchor's own offering** — the thing they sell, exactly as they sell it now.
2. **All seven prompts, in order, no skipping.** The prompt you want to skip is the one carrying your blind spot.
3. **Test against the Session 5 questions.** Can the owner do it, this month, with what they have?
4. **Eliminate again, harder.** What does this business do out of habit rather than need?

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Run all seven SCAMPER prompts against your anchor's own offering."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:55 · All seven, on the offering (30 min)

Printed grid. Your anchor's actual product or service. Minimum three ideas per row. Write in Marathi or Hindi — this is divergent work.

### 0:55 – 1:10 · Score with the Session 5 questions (15 min)

Owner can do it? This month? With what they have? Mark every idea. Do not discard the failures — mark them 'later'.

### 1:10 – 1:25 · Pick two and detail them (15 min)

Two surviving ideas. For each: what changes on Monday morning, and what does the customer notice?

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Ideas naming a specific customer behaviour that would change
- An Eliminate row with something genuinely uncomfortable in it
- A student who finds their Session 5 idea beaten by a SCAMPER output

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| All ideas need money | "Take the Eliminate row. Removing costs nothing." |
| Ideas are generic — 'improve quality' | "Improve it how, so the customer notices on their next visit?" |
| Refuses to change the core offering | "Nobody is closing the shop. What is one thing about it you could change and keep everything else?" |
| Reverts to their Session 5 idea for all seven rows | "Set that aside for twenty minutes. What else does the grid give you?" |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

---

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes. Pitch one of the two detailed ideas, and say what the customer notices.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.

---

## 1:50 – 2:00 · Idea log

Students fill the Session 12 page of `templates/idea_log.md`. Silent — do not talk over it.

> Did SCAMPER beat the idea you committed to in Session 5? Answer honestly.
