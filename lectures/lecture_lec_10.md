# Session 10 — Reading a Map — What It Tells You

**Unit:** 2 · Mind Mapping and Creative Thinking Techniques
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.4, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> Closes Unit 2. Students stop making maps and start diagnosing them — including their own attention.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Recall, then warm-up — "Read a Stranger's Map"

### 0:00 – 0:03 · Recall — from memory, log closed

Log closed, from memory, said aloud to a partner:

> What were your startup map's two thinnest branches? What question did you write for each?

Three minutes. Do not correct anyone; the effort of retrieving is the point, not accuracy. Retrieval from memory is what moves last session's learning from short-term to long-term — rereading the log does not.

### 0:03 – 0:10 · Warm-up

**What you do.**

Borrow one student's map, with permission. Pin it up. Read it aloud as a diagnosis, not a summary:

> "This side is crowded and this side is empty. Six branches about product, one about customers. What does that tell us about where this person's attention has been?"

**Why it is here.** Models the whole session in two minutes, on a real artefact rather than an example.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **A map is a picture of your attention. The empty side of the page is the finding.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **Density shows where you have been thinking**, not where the business needs work.
2. **A sparse branch is a question, not a gap.** It means you have not looked there.
3. **The missing branch is the blind spot** — the BOI you never drew at all.
4. **Buzan's numerical order.** Number your BOIs by importance and the map becomes an agenda.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Diagnose your own maps: where has your attention been, and what have you avoided?"

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:45 · Lay them all out (20 min)

Every map from Sessions 6 to 9, side by side. Which branches recur? Which appear once and never again? Write two lines on where your attention has concentrated.

### 0:45 – 1:05 · Draw the avoided branch (20 min)

There is a branch you have not drawn in four sessions. Money, or the owner's family, or a competitor, or the customer who stopped coming. Draw it now.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · One page on what the maps say (20 min)

Not what the business is — what your maps reveal about the business and about what you have been ignoring. Number your BOIs by importance.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- A student naming an avoidance honestly
- Recurring branches identified across four different maps
- A numbered BOI order that differs from the drawing order

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| "I have not avoided anything" | "Name your customer's biggest complaint. Which map has it?" |
| Summarises instead of diagnosing | "You told me what is on the map. Tell me what the map's shape means." |
| All four maps identical | "Then you settled early. What did you stop asking after Session 6?" |
| Cannot number BOIs | "Which one, fixed, makes the others easier? That is number one." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

### If someone finishes early

In creative work, finishing early usually means stopping too soon. Hand them this:

> Number your BOIs by importance, then again by ease. Where do the two orders disagree?

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes. Pitch the avoided branch — this is usually the strongest pitch of Unit 2 because it is the most honest.
