# Session 13 — Team SCAMPER

**Unit:** 3 · SCAMPER Technique for Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.3, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "Rapid Combine"

**What you do.**

> "Ninety seconds. I name two things, you combine them into a business. A barber and a tea stall. A tempo and a cold store. A coaching class and a canteen."

**Why it is here.** Warms the Combine prompt specifically — the one that benefits most from more than one head.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Run the prompts in silence first, then talk. Reverse that order and the loudest person's grid becomes the team's grid.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **Silent first, always.** Each member fills the whole grid alone before anyone speaks.
2. **Then merge, prompt by prompt.** Compare Substitute rows before anyone touches Combine.
3. **Disagreement means the row is alive.** Two different answers on one prompt is better than four identical ones.
4. **Pick the problem carefully.** Real, specific, and one nobody at the table has already solved.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Working in groups, apply SCAMPER to a real-world business or tech problem."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:35 · Choose the problem (10 min)

Teams of four. One real problem: a team member's anchor, or something on campus everybody experiences. Write it as a sentence before you start.

### 0:35 – 0:55 · Silent grids (20 min)

Every member fills all seven rows alone. No talking. Twenty minutes.

### 0:55 – 1:25 · Merge prompt by prompt (30 min)

Compare Substitute rows across all four grids, then Combine, and so on. Build one team grid. Rotate the scribe every two prompts.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Four visibly different grids before the merge
- A team arguing about one prompt for five minutes
- The scribe genuinely rotating

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Team talks during the silent phase | "Silent means silent. You get twenty-five minutes to argue afterwards." |
| Merged grid is one person's grid | "Whose Adapt row is this? Take one from each of the other three." |
| Problem chosen is too vague | "Who exactly has this problem, and when did you last see it happen?" |
| Team picks a problem they already solved | "You know the answer. Pick one you do not." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes, but **team-pitched** today — one member presents, the team answers critique together. Individual pitches resume in Session 14.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.

## If you are short on time

Cut the silent phase to fifteen minutes. Never merge before silence.

## Room size

- **Under 10 students:** everyone pitches; add a second critique round.
- **Over 40:** six pitch today. Track it and rotate — do not let the confident students take every slot.
