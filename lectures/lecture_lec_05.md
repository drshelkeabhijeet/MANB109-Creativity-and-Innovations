# Session 05 — From Many Ideas to One

**Unit:** 1 · Foundations of Creativity and Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.1, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> Closes Unit 1. The session is about subtraction, which students find far harder than generation and which no amount of brainstorming teaches.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Recall, then warm-up — "Kill Your Darling"

### 0:00 – 0:03 · Recall — from memory, log closed

Log closed, from memory, said aloud to a partner:

> What did your front-door audit find? What did you sketch, and which teammate's idea did you borrow?

Three minutes. Do not correct anyone; the effort of retrieving is the point, not accuracy. Retrieval from memory is what moves last session's learning from short-term to long-term — rereading the log does not.

### 0:03 – 0:10 · Warm-up

**What you do.**

> "Name your favourite idea so far. Now you have sixty seconds to argue against it. Convince me it is a bad idea."

Round the room fast. No defending afterwards.

**Why it is here.** Separates the student from the idea before the selection work starts. It is much easier to drop an idea you have already argued against once.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Choosing is harder than generating, and it is where most student projects quietly fail.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **You now have more ideas than time.** Four sessions of material. That is the good problem.
2. **Three questions, in order:** can the owner actually do this? Can they start this month? With what they already have?
3. **An idea that fails any of the three is not a bad idea** — it is a later idea. Write it down and set it aside.
4. **Commit.** From here to Session 15 you are developing one idea, not keeping five alive.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Choose the single idea you will carry to the end of the semester, and write the case for it in five sentences."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:45 · Everything you have (20 min)

Go through your idea log, Sessions 1 to 4. List every idea you have had for this anchor. Most students find between twelve and twenty.

### 0:45 – 1:05 · Score against the three questions (20 min)

Owner can do it? This month? With what they have? Three columns, yes or no. No maybes.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · The case, in five sentences (20 min)

Pick one. Five sentences: what it is, who it helps, why now, what it costs, what happens if it works.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- A student who drops the idea they arrived with
- Five sentences containing a number
- Someone who picks a small idea over an impressive one

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Cannot choose between two | "Which one could you start on Monday? Take that one. Write the other in your log." |
| Chooses the most impressive idea | "Can the owner do this without money they do not have? Score it honestly." |
| All ideas fail all three questions | "Then your ideas are for a different business. What could this owner do this week?" |
| Five sentences with no numbers | "How many customers? How many rupees? Put one number in." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

### If someone finishes early

In creative work, finishing early usually means stopping too soon. Hand them this:

> Write the five-sentence case for the idea you killed. If it comes out stronger, swap.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

**First pitch of a single converged idea.** Full structure, 3 minutes. This is the pitch Unit 1 has been building to — the critique should be tougher, and say so beforehand.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.

---

## 1:50 – 2:00 · Idea log

Students fill the Session 5 page of `templates/idea_log.md`. Silent — do not talk over it.

> Write down the idea you killed that you still like. You may come back to it in Unit 3.

## Before Session 6

**Show your owner the five-sentence case before Session 6.** Write down their exact first reaction — the words they used, not your summary. That reaction is Session 6's opening.

---

## If you are short on time

Cut the listing round. Never cut the five sentences.

## Room size

- **Under 10 students:** everyone pitches; add a second critique round.
- **Over 40:** six pitch today. Track it and rotate — do not let the confident students take every slot.
