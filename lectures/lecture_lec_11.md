# Session 11 — SCAMPER — Seven Ways to Change a Thing

**Unit:** 3 · SCAMPER Technique for Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.3, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> Unit 3 opens. SCAMPER is presented as a checklist that works on a bad day — the opposite of waiting for inspiration.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "Change the Chai"

**What you do.**

Hold up a cup of tea, or describe one.

> "Seven prompts, thirty seconds each. Substitute something. Combine it with something. Adapt it from somewhere else. Modify it. Put it to another use. Eliminate something. Rearrange it."

Shout answers. Keep it fast and silly.

**Why it is here.** Runs the full method in three and a half minutes on something with no stakes, so the structure is familiar before it meets the anchor.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **SCAMPER is not inspiration. It is seven questions asked in order, and it works even on a day when you feel like you have no ideas.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The seven prompts.** Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Rearrange.
2. **Order matters less than completeness.** Running all seven badly beats running two well — the value is in coverage.
3. **Eliminate is the most productive and the least used.** People add. Removing is where the surprises are.
4. **Write before you judge.** Fill all seven rows first. Evaluate afterwards, never during.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Run all seven prompts on a physical object in this room, then on one you chose."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:40 · Worked demo together (15 min)

One object everyone can see — a steel tiffin box, a college ID card, an autorickshaw. All seven prompts, called out, written on the board. You facilitate; the room supplies the ideas.

### 0:40 – 1:05 · All seven, on your own object (25 min)

Each student picks any object in the room or in their bag. Printed SCAMPER grid, seven rows, minimum three ideas per row. Twenty-one ideas minimum.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · Trade and extend (20 min)

Swap grids. Add one idea to each of your partner's seven rows. You will find their empty rows easily — everyone has one.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Grids with all seven rows filled, even weakly
- A student who gets stuck on Eliminate and pushes through
- Partners spotting the row that was skipped

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Skips Eliminate | "What could you remove and still have it work? Now what if you removed the most important part?" |
| Confuses Adapt and Modify | "Adapt borrows from elsewhere. Modify changes what is already there. Give me one of each." |
| One idea per row and stops | "Three per row. The third is the one worth having." |
| Judging while writing | "No crossing out yet. Fill the grid, judge after." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes. Pitch your best single SCAMPER output and name which prompt produced it.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.
