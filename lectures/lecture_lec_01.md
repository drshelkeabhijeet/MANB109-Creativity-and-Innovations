# Session 01 — What Creativity Is, and Why Your Ideas Get Stuck

**Unit:** 1 · Foundations of Creativity and Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.1, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> First meeting of the semester. It has two jobs: make the room safe enough to say a bad
> idea out loud, and get every student committed to one real business they will work on for
> fifteen weeks. Content is secondary today — if students leave having said something silly
> in public and chosen an anchor, the session worked.

---

## Before the room

- [ ] Printed, one per student: `templates/anchor_business_brief.md`
- [ ] Printed, one per student: `templates/pitch_structure.md` (they read it, don't use it fully today)
- [ ] Students told to bring a notebook for the idea log — any cheap notebook
- [ ] Board or wall space, markers
- [ ] *No projector required.* If one is available it adds nothing to this session.

---

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "The Worst Possible Idea"

**What you do.** Groups of four. Read this out:

> "You have three minutes. The question is: **how could we make absolutely certain that
> nobody ever buys a cup of tea at the campus canteen again?** The worse the idea, the
> better. Shout them out — I want to hear laughing."

Let it run loud. Write the worst four on the board.

Then, at 7 minutes:

> "Now pick your worst one. Turn it upside down. What is the *good* idea hiding inside it?"

**Why it is here.** The first public act of the semester is being deliberately bad, which is
the safest possible entry — you cannot fail at being wrong on purpose. It also demonstrates
the session's actual point before you say it: what stops ideas is judgement, not ability.

> Weeks 1–2 are **team-attributed**. Never write a student's name next to an idea yet.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Creativity is having ideas. Innovation is an idea that actually reaches somebody.
> This course is about getting from the first to the second.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The distinction.** A tea stall owner invents a new masala — that is creativity. Forty
   people a day buy it and come back — that is innovation. One is private; the other
   changed something for somebody.
2. **Ideas have a lifecycle, and most die in the middle.** Between "I thought of it" and
   "someone used it" there is a long gap where ideas quietly disappear. We will spend the
   semester in that gap.
3. **Four barriers, and none of them is about talent.** Judging too early. Fear of looking
   foolish in front of people you know. "We have always done it this way." And the expertise
   trap — knowing so well how a thing is done that you cannot see it done differently.
4. **These are predictable, so they are beatable.** Mind mapping beats the second and third.
   SCAMPER beats the fourth. Today's warm-up beat the first. That is the whole course.

**Do not** cover the history of creativity research, or list every named creative-process
model. Fifteen minutes, then stop. If it will not fit, it belongs in the making phase as
something students discover.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making — Your anchor, and twenty questions

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Pick the one real business you are going to work on for the whole semester. Then write
> twenty questions you would need to ask its owner."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi. This is divergent work — write fast in the language you
think in. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:40 · Choose it (15 min)

Hand out `templates/anchor_business_brief.md`. Fill sections 1 and 2 from what you already
know. Blanks are expected — you have not visited yet.

Say this plainly:

> "Do not pick the most interesting business. Pick the one whose owner will actually pick up
> your phone call. You have fifteen weeks with this choice."

### 0:40 – 1:00 · Twenty questions (20 min)

> "Twenty questions you would need to ask the owner. Questions, not answers. I want quantity —
> twenty rough ones, not five polished ones."

Twenty is deliberately more than feels comfortable. The first eight are obvious, the next
seven are effortful, and the last five are where the interesting ones live.

### 1:00 – 1:25 · Swap and sharpen (25 min)

Pairs exchange briefs. Your partner:
- **circles the three sharpest** questions
- **crosses out the five weakest**
- explains both choices aloud

Then each student marks their three sharpest — these are the ones they will actually ask.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Questions beginning *why* and *what happens when*, not only *how many*
- A student who changes their business halfway through — that is engagement, not indecision
- Questions the student genuinely cannot answer themselves

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Blank sheet after 5 minutes | "Tell me about the owner. Not the business — the person." |
| Only countable questions (price, customers, hours) | "Those you can count from outside. Ask me one you could only get by listening." |
| Fewer than ten questions at the 15-minute mark | "Write ten bad ones fast. We cut them in the next round." |
| Picks Amazon, Zomato, a big brand | "Can you visit them this week? No? Then pick again." |
| Two students pick the same shop | Fine — let them. They will produce different maps, and that comparison is useful in Unit 2. |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

First pitch of the semester — **shortened and team-attributed**, per the weeks 1–2 rule.

- Each team of four nominates **one brief**. The team presents it, not the individual.
- **Two minutes:** who is this business, and what is the sharpest question we found?
- One round of critique using `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — **you model the first
  full set of three moves yourself**, then hand it to the room.

Full three-minute structure from `templates/pitch_structure.md` begins in Session 2. Hand the
sheet out today so they can read it before then.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If you run out of time and cut
> it, the CO is not met. Cut the input instead.

---

## 1:50 – 2:00 · Idea log

Students fill the Session 1 page of `templates/idea_log.md`. Silent — do not talk over it.

Say this before they start:

> "Box 4 asks what you now think about your business. In week fifteen you will read what you
> wrote today. Write something you are willing to be wrong about."

## Forward link — say it out loud

> "The twenty-questions habit is exactly the move you will use in **MANB307A, Case Studies in
> Agri-Business Management**, when you open a case for the first time. A case is just a
> business whose owner is not in the room to answer you."

---

## Before Session 2

Visit or phone your anchor enterprise. Ask your **three sharpest questions**. Bring the
answers — in any language — to the next session. That is the only work between sessions.

---

## If you are short on time

Cut the input to ten minutes. **Never** cut the twenty questions or the closing pitch.

## Room size

- **Under 10 students:** every team pitches, and there is time for a second critique round.
- **Over 40:** six teams pitch today. Track who has presented and rotate — do not let the
  confident students absorb every slot across the semester.
