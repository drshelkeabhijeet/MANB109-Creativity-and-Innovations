## 0:00 – 0:10 · Recall, then warm-up — "What Must Exist"

### 0:00 – 0:03 · Recall — from memory, log closed

Log closed, from memory, said aloud to a partner:

> What branch did your team draw on your anchor that you would not have drawn yourself?

Three minutes. Do not correct anyone; the effort of retrieving is the point, not accuracy. Retrieval from memory is what moves last session's learning from short-term to long-term — rereading the log does not.

### 0:03 – 0:10 · Warm-up

**What you do.**

> "Ninety seconds. Shout out everything a business needs to exist before it sells anything."

Write them on the board. You will get twenty or so — offering, customer, price, place, money, licence, staff, phone number.

**Why it is here.** Builds the BOI set for the session's map collectively, so nobody starts from a blank centre.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **A startup is not an idea. It is a list of things that must all exist at the same time.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **Launch components as BOIs.** Offering, customer, channel, money, people, legal, technology. Four to seven of these become the main branches.
2. **The map exposes what you have not thought about.** A thin branch is not a small problem; it is an unexamined one.
3. **Tie it to your anchor.** The startup is either the digital version of what your owner already does, or the adjacent thing they could start.
4. **Buzan's laws still apply.** Central image, one word per line, curved branches, landscape.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Create a mind map for a technology startup idea, visually breaking down the components needed to launch it."

> **Prescribed activity, verbatim from the syllabus:** *Create a mind map for a technology startup idea, visually breaking down the components needed to launch it.*

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:35 · Choose the venture (10 min)

The digital version of your anchor's business, or an adjacent venture its owner could launch. Write one sentence naming it before you draw.

### 0:35 – 1:10 · Map it (35 min)

A3 landscape, Buzan's laws. Central image of the venture. BOIs from the board. Split each until you hit things that either exist or must be built.

### 1:10 – 1:25 · Mark the thin branches (15 min)

Circle the two thinnest branches. Those are what you have not thought about — write one question you would need answered for each.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- A venture recognisably connected to the anchor
- Branches naming specific things rather than categories
- A student circling a thin branch that is genuinely the hard part

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Venture is unrelated to the anchor | "Fine, but harder — you have no owner to ask. Can you connect it back?" |
| Map is all technology | "Who pays? Where do they find you? Two more BOIs." |
| Every branch equally thick | "Something here you know least about. Which branch did you fill from imagination?" |
| Copying the board list as branches | "Those are the buckets. What goes inside yours specifically?" |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

### If someone finishes early

In creative work, finishing early usually means stopping too soon. Hand them this:

> Take your thinnest branch and grow it three levels. What do you now need to find out?

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes. Pitch the venture, then point at the thinnest branch — beat 5, the ask, is what you need to find out.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.
