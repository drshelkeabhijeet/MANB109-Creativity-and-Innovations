# Session 02 — The Creative Process, Start to Finish

**Unit:** 1 · Foundations of Creativity and Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.1, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> Students return with answers from their first owner conversation. The session turns that raw material into a located problem: not 'sales are down' but 'the owner is stuck at verification — she has ideas but no way to test one cheaply'.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "30 Circles"

**What you do.**

Hand out a sheet of thirty empty circles. Five minutes: turn each circle into a recognisable object. Count them at the end — most students get eight to fifteen.

> "Who got more than twenty? Nobody. Now look at your neighbour's. Did anyone think of a face? A wheel? Almost everyone did. The first ideas we all reach for are the same ideas."

**Why it is here.** Shows fluency and originality as two different things, in ninety seconds of evidence rather than a definition.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **The good idea is not the hard part. The hard part is everything that happens after it.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The stages.** Preparation, incubation, illumination, verification, implementation. Illumination is the one everybody romanticises and the one that takes the least time.
2. **Incubation is real work.** The idea that arrives in the bus is paid for by the two hours you spent stuck the day before.
3. **Where ideas actually die.** Two gaps: between illumination and verification (no cheap way to test), and between verification and implementation (it works, nobody does it).
4. **Locating the stall.** Your anchor's owner is stuck at exactly one of these stages. Naming which one is today's work.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Find out which stage your anchor's owner is stuck at, and write the problem in one sentence you could act on."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:40 · Visit debrief, in pairs (15 min)

Tell your partner one thing the owner said that you did not expect. Not the useful thing — the surprising one. Partners write down the surprise for you; you are too close to hear it.

### 0:40 – 1:00 · Locate the stall (20 min)

Which stage is the owner stuck at? Preparation (does not know enough about the customer)? Illumination (no ideas)? Verification (cannot test cheaply)? Implementation (knows what to do, cannot start)? Mark it and write two lines of evidence from the visit.

### 1:00 – 1:25 · The sentence, three times (25 min)

Write the problem in one sentence. Then rewrite it more specifically. Then again. The third version is usually the first one you can act on.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- A surprise the student did not expect — that is a sign the owner actually talked
- A problem sentence that names a person, not a category
- A student who realises the stated problem was not the real one

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| "The owner said everything is fine" | "People say that to guests. What did they complain about while saying it?" |
| Problem sentence is 'sales are down' | "Down from what, since when, and for which customer? Cut that sentence in half and make it specific." |
| Could not reach the owner | "Pair with someone who did. Work their anchor today, phone yours before Session 3." |
| Locates the stall at every stage at once | "Pick the one that, if fixed, makes the others easier." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.
