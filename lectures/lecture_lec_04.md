## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "The Worst Website"

**What you do.**

Callback to Session 1.

> "Three minutes. Describe the worst possible website for your anchor business. What is on it? What happens when a customer taps it?"

Take the four worst aloud.

**Why it is here.** Reuses the safest move in the course at the point where students are about to be asked for their best ideas. Also surfaces real failure modes — students describe things they have genuinely encountered.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Your customer's first contact with this business is already digital, whether the owner designed it or not.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The front door is rarely a website.** For most businesses here it is a WhatsApp number, a Google Maps pin, or a photo someone else uploaded.
2. **It exists already, unmanaged.** Search your anchor right now — whatever comes up is the front door, including nothing at all.
3. **Redesign means the whole first contact**, not a homepage: what a customer sees, how they ask, how they pay, how they come back.
4. **Cheap beats impressive.** A correct Google listing with photos and real hours outperforms a website nobody visits.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Brainstorm ideas to redesign a basic mobile app or website and create a creative solution — for your anchor's own digital front door."

> **Prescribed activity, verbatim from the syllabus:** *Brainstorm ideas to redesign a basic mobile app or website and create a creative solution.*

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:35 · Audit the front door (10 min)

Search your anchor on Google Maps and WhatsApp. What exists? Wrong hours, no photos, nothing at all? Write what a first-time customer would see. No phone: describe it from memory and mark it unverified.

### 0:35 – 1:05 · Team brainstorm — quantity target (30 min)

Teams of four. Target twenty-five ideas across the team for redesigning the front door. No judging, no discussion, write everything. Count aloud at the end.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · Sketch your one (20 min)

Each student takes one idea — yours or a teammate's — and sketches the redesigned front door on A3. A drawing, not a specification.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Teams that hit twenty-five and keep going
- A sketch showing what the customer sees, not what the owner administers
- Students who discover their anchor is already listed with wrong information

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Team stalls at ten ideas | "Ten more, all bad ones. Go." |
| Everything is 'build a website' | "Nobody is visiting a website for a shop they can walk to. What happens on WhatsApp?" |
| No smartphone in the team | "Describe what you remember seeing. Mark it unverified and check before next week." |
| Sketching a screen with forty features | "A customer gives this six seconds. Cross out everything they do not need in six seconds." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes, individual. Hold the A3 sketch up during beat 4 — this is the first session where the artefact does real work in the pitch.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.
