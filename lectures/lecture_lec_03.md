# Session 03 — Breaking the Blocks

**Unit:** 1 · Foundations of Creativity and Innovation
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.1, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> First individual pitch. The technique — assumption reversal — is deliberately paired with the session where students stop hiding behind their team.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Recall, then warm-up — "Forced Connection"

### 0:00 – 0:03 · Recall — from memory, log closed

Log closed, from memory, said aloud to a partner:

> Which stage is your owner stuck at? What was your evidence? Say the third version of your problem sentence.

Three minutes. Do not correct anyone; the effort of retrieving is the point, not accuracy. Retrieval from memory is what moves last session's learning from short-term to long-term — rereading the log does not.

### 0:03 – 0:10 · Warm-up

**What you do.**

Name a random object: a bicycle, a pressure cooker, a temple bell. Three minutes.

> "How is your anchor business like a bicycle? Give me three ways. They do not have to be sensible."

Take four answers aloud. Take the strangest one seriously for thirty seconds.

**Why it is here.** Forcing an unrelated object into the frame breaks the expertise trap from Session 1 — it makes the familiar business briefly unfamiliar.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **Every business runs on assumptions nobody has questioned in years. Your job is to find one and turn it upside down.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **Assumptions are invisible from inside.** The owner cannot see them because they are the water. You can, because you arrived last week.
2. **Reversal, not criticism.** 'Customers want low prices' becomes 'what if they wanted high prices?' — you are not saying the owner is wrong, you are testing whether the rule is load-bearing.
3. **Most reversals fail, and that is fine.** Nine will be nonsense. The tenth reorganises the business.
4. **The one that scares you is the signal.** If a reversal makes you uncomfortable, it is touching something real.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "List ten things your anchor's owner believes must be true. Then turn each one upside down."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:45 · Ten assumptions (20 min)

What does the owner hold as fixed? 'We must open at eight.' 'Customers will not pay for delivery.' 'Nobody wants this in a smaller size.' Ten of them. Write fast.

### 0:45 – 1:05 · Reverse every one (20 min)

For each: what if the opposite were true? Do not judge yet. Write the reversal even when it is absurd.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · The one that scares you (20 min)

Pick the reversal you least want to be true. Sketch, in words or a rough drawing, what the business looks like if it is.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Assumptions phrased as the owner would say them, in their words
- A reversal that makes the student laugh and then go quiet
- Students borrowing a reversal from a neighbour's very different business

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Assumptions are all about money | "Give me three that have nothing to do with price." |
| Reversals are all 'the opposite is bad' | "You are arguing with it. Just state it and move on — judge later." |
| Fewer than six assumptions | "What time do they open? Why that time? There is one." |
| Picks a safe reversal for the last step | "That one is comfortable. Which one did you skip?" |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

### If someone finishes early

In creative work, finishing early usually means stopping too soon. Hand them this:

> Take your scariest reversal. List three things that would have to be true for it to work.

## If you are short on time

Cut the reversal round to fifteen minutes. Never cut the scary one.

## Room size

- **Under 10 students:** everyone pitches; add a second critique round.
- **Over 40:** six pitch today. Track it and rotate — do not let the confident students take every slot.
