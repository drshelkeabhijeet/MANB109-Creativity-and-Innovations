## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "Six Seconds"

**What you do.**

> "Describe an app or website you abandoned in the first six seconds. Why?"

Take six answers fast. Write the reasons on the board — they are the redesign criteria for today.

**Why it is here.** Generates the evaluation criteria from the students' own experience rather than handing them a heuristics list.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **A redesign is not a new coat of paint. It is deciding what the thing is for, and removing what does not serve that.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **The object is a tech product** — an app or a website. Your anchor's front door from Session 4, or a product everyone at the table uses.
2. **All seven prompts, as a team, silent first.** Same discipline as last session.
3. **Eliminate carries this session.** Look at the board — most abandonment reasons are about too much, not too little.
4. **Produce something visible.** A drawn screen, not a description.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Teams apply SCAMPER to redesign or enhance a tech product (for example, a mobile app or website)."

> **Prescribed activity, verbatim from the syllabus:** *Teams apply SCAMPER to redesign or enhance a tech product (e.g., a mobile app or website).*

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:35 · Choose and frame (10 min)

Teams of four. One tech product: a member's anchor front door, or an app everyone uses. Write in one sentence what it is *for*.

### 0:35 – 1:05 · Silent grids, then merge (30 min)

All seven prompts alone for twelve minutes, then merge prompt by prompt into one team grid. Rotate the scribe.

### 1:05 – 1:25 · Draw the redesign (20 min)

A3. Draw the redesigned screen or front door. Mark which SCAMPER prompt produced each change.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- Changes traceable to a named prompt
- A redesign that removes more than it adds
- A team checking their drawing against the board's abandonment reasons

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Redesign only adds features | "Your board says people leave because there is too much. What did you remove?" |
| Cannot agree on what it is for | "That disagreement is the finding. Write both, pick one for today." |
| Drawing is a specification | "Draw what the customer sees in the first six seconds. Nothing else." |
| Team splits into two designs | "Build both, pitch both. Let the room choose." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

---

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes, individual again. Hold the A3 up and name the prompt behind your favourite change.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.

> Note the change you argued for that the team rejected.
