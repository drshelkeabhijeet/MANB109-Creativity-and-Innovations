# Session 06 — Mind Mapping — Buzan's Laws

**Unit:** 2 · Mind Mapping and Creative Thinking Techniques
**Duration:** 120 min · **COs:** 109.4, 109.2
**Session type:** Studio (practical)

> Unit 2 opens. Mind mapping here is Tony Buzan's method specifically, not a generic bubble diagram — the laws are what make it work, and students will break most of them on their first attempt by design.

## 0:00 – 0:10 · Warm-up — "Map Your Morning — With No Rules"

**What you do.**

> "Five minutes. Map everything you did this morning. However you like — I am giving you no rules."

Collect nothing. Do not comment. Move straight into the input; the maps come back at the end of it.

**Why it is here.** Students produce a lopsided list-with-boxes. When Buzan's laws land ten minutes later, they can see exactly which ones they broke and why it hurt the map.

---

## 0:10 – 0:25 · Input

**The one idea students must leave with:**

> **A list hides relationships. A Buzan map shows them — and the laws are what make the difference between a map and a doodle.**

**Say roughly this — four beats, not a lecture:**

1. **Radiant thinking.** The brain does not store in lines; it radiates from a centre. A map on paper copies that shape, which is why recall improves.
2. **The laws of emphasis.** A central *image*, not a word. Three colours minimum in that image. Branch thickness heaviest at the centre and thinning outward. Varying letter size.
3. **The laws of clarity.** ONE key word per line. Print, do not join up. The word sits *on* the line. Line length equals word length. Curved branches, never straight rulered ones. Paper **landscape**.
4. **Basic Ordering Ideas.** The main branches off the centre are your BOIs — the four to seven big buckets. Get those right and the rest of the map organises itself.

**Do not** run past fifteen minutes. If it will not fit, move it into the making phase as something students discover rather than something you tell them.

---

## 0:25 – 1:25 · Making

**The task, as you will state it:**

> "Draw a Buzan-compliant map of your anchor enterprise, twice."

**Language:** Marathi or Hindi for divergent work. Only the pitch is in English.

### 0:25 – 0:35 · Check your morning map against the laws (10 min)

Hold up your warm-up map. How many laws did it break? Almost everyone writes sentences, uses straight lines, and starts with a word instead of an image. Nobody is in trouble — that is the point of the exercise.

### 0:35 – 1:00 · First map of the anchor (25 min)

A3, landscape. Central IMAGE of the business — draw it, do not write the name. Three colours in that image. Four to seven BOIs as curved main branches, one key word each, printed on the line. Then radiate.

### 1:00 – 1:25 · Draw it again (25 min)

Second map, fresh sheet. The second map is always better because the BOIs are now obvious. Keep both.

### Circulating — what good looks like

- A drawn central image, however rough — a stick-figure shopfront counts
- Curved branches thickening toward the centre
- One word per line, held all the way to the third level
- A second map with different BOIs from the first

### What stuck looks like, and the nudge

| If you see | Say |
|---|---|
| Writes the business name in the centre | "Draw it. Badly is fine. Buzan's first law is an image, and it is first for a reason." |
| Sentences on branches | "One word. Which single word carries that whole phrase?" |
| Straight ruled lines | "Curve them. The eye follows a curve and skips a straight line — that is the whole reason for the law." |
| "I cannot draw" | "Neither can I. A circle with a door is a shop. Nobody is marking the drawing." |
| Paper in portrait | "Turn it landscape. You will run out of room on the right otherwise." |
| Fifteen main branches | "Those are sub-branches. Which four to seven buckets do they belong under? Those are your BOIs." |

**Do not solve it for them.** Ask a question and walk away.

## 1:25 – 1:50 · Critique and pitch

3 minutes, individual. Hold the second map up during beat 4 and point at one branch — do not read the map aloud.

Run `templates/peer_critique_protocol.md` — I like / I wish / What if.

> This closing beat is the attainment path for **CO 109.2**. If time runs short, cut the input, never the pitch.

## If you are short on time

Cut the second map to fifteen minutes. Never skip it entirely — the redraw is where the learning is.

## Room size

- **Under 10 students:** everyone pitches; add a second critique round.
- **Over 40:** six pitch today. Track it and rotate — do not let the confident students take every slot.
